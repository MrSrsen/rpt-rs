# Meridian Global Logistics — the synthetic reporting corpus

> **Everything here is fictional.** Meridian Global Logistics (MGL) is an invented company created solely to give
> `rpt-rs` a single, coherent, deterministic dataset to build realistic Crystal Reports against. No real companies,
> people, customers, or data are represented. Safe to commit and publish.

This directory documents the synthetic company and its database. It is the reference the report authors bind against and
the reader's guide to what the numbers *mean*.

- **[`README.md`](README.md)** (this file) — the company story: what MGL is and does.
- **[`SCHEMA.md`](SCHEMA.md)** — every table and field, with descriptions and how each feeds the reports/charts.

Related locations:
- **Seed database:** `tests/meridian/sql/meridian.sql` (deterministic, generated by `apps/meridian-seed`).
- **Report files:** `tests/meridian/reports/` (the `.rpt` files, organized by division).
- **HTML baselines:** `tests/meridian/baselines/html/` (mirrors each report's path under `reports/`, `.rpt`→`.html`).

## The company

**Meridian Global Logistics (MGL)** is a mid-sized, privately-held third-party logistics (3PL) group. Founded in **1987**
in **Rotterdam** by a group of freight forwarders who wanted to combine physical transport with merchandise
distribution, MGL grew from a single North Sea warehouse into a network spanning **five world regions** (the Americas,
EMEA, Asia-Pacific, Latin America, and the Middle East & Africa). Today it employs a few hundred people across sales,
operations, fleet, and capital projects, and it does two things that reinforce each other:

1. **It moves goods** — a physical logistics network of warehouses, hubs, ports, and terminals, served by an owned fleet
   plus contracted carriers, across four transport modes (**sea, air, road, rail**).
2. **It trades goods** — MGL sources merchandise from a global supplier base, holds it in its own facilities, and sells
   it to business customers, invoicing in the customer's local currency.

This dual nature is deliberate: it means one company's data naturally contains both *freight-style* reporting (geography,
shipment tracking, fuel prices, carrier performance, capital-project schedules) and *merchandise-style* reporting
(product catalogs, customer statements, multi-currency invoices, sales analysis) — the full range of report shapes a
Crystal Reports engine must handle.

### The three divisions

- **Meridian Freight** — the physical network. Books and executes shipments across the hub graph, splitting each into
  one or more legs (an ocean leg, a road leg, etc.), each handled by a carrier and a vehicle. Every shipment emits a
  stream of **tracking events** (booked → picked up → departed → in transit → customs → arrived → delivered), which is
  the company's highest-volume data. Freight also tracks **fuel prices** (which drive fuel surcharges) and grades its
  carriers monthly on a **scorecard**.

- **Meridian Trade** — the merchandise side. Maintains a **product catalog** organized as a category hierarchy
  (division → group → class), sourced from **suppliers** worldwide. Sells to **customers** segmented by industry, tier,
  and geography; takes **sales orders**, fulfils them, and raises **invoices** and records **payments** — all
  multi-currency, reconciled through daily **exchange rates**.

- **Meridian Capital Projects** — builds and expands the network: new distribution centers, port terminals, cross-dock
  hubs. Each project is a schedule of dependency-linked **tasks** with planned/actual dates and percent-complete — the
  data behind project Gantt charts.

### Scale & shape of the business (what makes the reports realistic)

The generated data is drawn from realistic, deliberately-skewed distributions (see `SCHEMA.md`), so the reports
tell a believable story rather than showing flat noise:

- **A few strategic accounts dominate revenue** (a Pareto ~80/20 split) — so Top-N customer reports and grand-total
  percentages are meaningful.
- **Q4 is peak season** — order and shipment volumes surge in the fourth quarter, visible in every trend/line chart.
- **A mid-year fuel-price spike** ripples through fuel surcharges and margins — visible in the fuel OHLC (stock) chart
  and surcharge-revenue reports.
- **One carrier chronically under-performs** on on-time delivery — the standout on the carrier radar/gauge scorecard.
- **A multi-year distribution-center expansion program** runs in the background — the capital-projects Gantt.
- **Transit times are right-skewed** — most shipments arrive on a typical schedule, with a long tail of delayed ones (a
  realistic transit-time histogram).

### Data tiers

The same seeded generator produces two volumes of the *same* dataset (same seed → identical rows at a given tier):

- **small** (~10k rows in the largest table) — committed to the repo, drives fast CI render tests.
- **large** (~1M rows in the largest table) — generated on demand for performance testing (query generation, DB fetch,
  the data pipeline, and pagination at scale).

See `SCHEMA.md` for the full table and field catalog.
